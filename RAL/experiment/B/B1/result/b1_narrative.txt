-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
METHODS — Adversarial sarcasm stress test (contextual rewrite)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

To test whether valence readout reflects pragmatic intent rather than shallow
lexical statistics, we construct an adversarial stress test from iSarcasmEval
SubTask C (200 English sarcasm–rephrase pairs). Each original pair is manually
rewritten into a contextual triplet under a six-step protocol
(see rewrite_protocol.md):

  (1) Intent extraction — identify the shared speaker attitude (e.g., frustration
      at bad weather) and hold it fixed across all three sentences.
  (2) De-culturation — replace culture-bound references (named politicians,
      sports teams, brands, local institutions) with generic roles and settings
      understandable at CEFR B1 English without domain knowledge.
  (3) Context injection — prepend one situational sentence (setting + observable
      trigger) before the quoted utterance so the model receives enough
      pragmatics to disambiguate sarcasm.
  (4) Triplet construction — three surface forms with controlled polarity:
        S (sarcastic):  positive wording, negative intent, adverse trigger;
        P (positive):   positive wording, positive intent, favourable trigger;
        N (negative):   negative wording, negative intent, adverse trigger.
      Sarcastic and sincere-positive sentences maximise lexical overlap on
      emotion-bearing tokens (e.g., both contain “lovely”) so that token-level
      comparisons isolate context from vocabulary change.
  (5) Keyword anchoring — annotate 1–3 emotion keywords per item for token-level
      V/A/D readout.
  (6) Quality control — verify intent consistency, absence of proper names, and
      positive surface on S relative to N.

All 200 rewritten triplets are released as context_triplets_en.json. Projection
uses the same forward pipeline as the rest of the paper: Qwen2.5-0.5B hidden
states, E1 English pole-50 valence/arousal/dominance axes, and AdaptiveScaler
parameters fit on the E1 training corpus. Sentence-level scores use the final
token; keyword scores use the hidden state at the anchored token.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULTS — Sentence-level decoupling from surface positivity
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

On the bare source sentences (without contextual rewrite), a 10-pair development
sample showed sarcastic utterances projecting strongly positive mean valence
(V̄_S ≈ 0.42) and remaining well above their literal-negative rephrases
(Δ(S−N) ≈ +0.41), indicating sensitivity to surface-positive words.

After applying the contextual rewrite to all 200 pairs, sentence-level valence
readout shifts markedly toward pragmatic alignment. Mean projected valence is
V̄_S = 0.46, V̄_P = 0.65, and V̄_N = 0.40. The sarcastic–negative gap collapses
to Δ(S−N) = +0.06, and in 81.5% of triplets the two conditions differ by less
than 0.15 in valence. Sarcastic sentences therefore co-locate with literal-negative
paraphrases at the sentence level once situational context is provided, while
remaining below sincere-positive counterparts (Δ(S−P) = −0.19).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULTS — Token-level context sensitivity at anchored keywords
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

To separate lexical lookup from contextual modulation, we compare valence at the
same emotion keyword across conditions. For keywords appearing in both sarcastic
and sincere-positive sentences (n = 325 token hits), mean valence is lower in
sarcastic context than in sincere-positive context: ΔV(S−P) = −0.20. The effect
is consistent for the primary keyword of each triplet (ΔV = −0.22, n = 200).
Arousal and dominance show smaller shifts (|Δ| < 0.07), supporting valence as
the primary axis for this pragmatics test.

Taken together, the stress test shows two complementary findings: (i) with brief
situational context, sentence-level valence of sarcastic text aligns with
literal-negative intent rather than with surface positivity; and (ii) at matched
positive lexemes, hidden-state projection encodes context-dependent valence
reduction, arguing against a pure dictionary-statistics explanation of the readout.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Suggested one-sentence result line for Abstract / Introduction
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

On 200 contextually rewritten sarcasm triplets, sarcastic and literal-negative
sentences yield overlapping valence (81.5% within ±0.15), while identical
positive keywords project lower valence under sarcastic than under sincere
context (ΔV = −0.20), demonstrating context-sensitive affect readout.
